About the Data

This dashboard shows Australian road-crash hospitalisations from 2011–2021, using BITRE’s September 2023 release based on AIHW hospital data.

Figures describe hospitalisation cases, not unique people or crashes.

View the BITRE dataset and documentation (opens in a new tab)

National Overview

Explore national changes over time and compare hospitalisations across road-user and age groups.

  • Total hospitalisations: recorded number of hospitalisation cases.
  • Average bed days per case: total bed days divided by hospitalisations.
  • Time-series breaks: Admission-policy changes in Victoria (2012) and NSW (2017) affect comparisons over time.
  • Changes in bed-day calculation methods also require caution when comparing years.

State Comparisons

Compare hospitalisation trends, road-user composition and age patterns across Australian states and territories.

  • Counts vs. rates: Counts show total hospitalisation volume. Rates per 100,000 account for population size: hospitalisations ÷ population × 100,000.
  • 2021 snapshot: The rate comparison uses 2021 hospitalisations and ABS 2021 Census population counts.
  • Rates account for population size, but not how much people travel.
  • Time-series breaks: Admission-policy changes in Victoria (2012) and NSW (2017) affect the respective jurisdiction trends.

The assignment brief states, “You are not required to normalise this data to pass this assessment.” Our team includes the 2021 rate comparison to account for population-size differences.

Population Comparison

Compare First Nations peoples and non-Indigenous Australians by age, counterparty and road-user hospitalisations.

  • Age and counterparty percentages describe hospitalisations within each population and selected year. They are not population injury rates.
  • Counterparty identifies the other party or object involved, not who caused the crash.
  • Car occupants includes drivers, passengers and occupants whose position was unknown.
  • Complete age percentages are unavailable for 2014 because some counts were not published.
  • “Not published” means unavailable—not zero. Annual counts require published values for both half-years.
